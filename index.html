FULL NINE MEN’S MORRIS

Nine pieces.
Every move matters.

Two players, nine pieces each, 24 points on three connected squares. Amber starts the first round; the starting player alternates on rematches.

1. Place all eighteen pieces.

Take turns putting one piece from your hand on any empty point. Captures do not return pieces to your hand. Neither player moves existing pieces until all eighteen have been placed.

2. Form a mill, then capture.

Three of your pieces along one of the board’s marked straight lines form a mill. Remove one opposing piece before your turn ends. A piece inside a mill is protected while any of that player’s pieces lie outside mills. If all their pieces are in mills, any may be taken.

Closing two mills with one action still earns one capture. You may open a mill and close it again on a later turn to earn another capture. Keeping an existing mill does not earn extra captures. A mill is not an immediate win.

3. Slide, then fly.

After placement, move one piece along a connecting line to the next empty point. No jumping over pieces, diagonal moves, or crossing the empty center. When reduced to exactly three pieces, you may fly to any empty point. This edition enables that common rules variation for both players.

Win or draw.

You win when your opponent has fewer than three pieces remaining, counting any unplaced pieces, or has no legal move after placement. Captures must be completed before checking the next turn.

This edition automatically draws after three repetitions of the same board and player-to-move, or 50 moves by each player without a capture. These explicit draw rules prevent endless rounds.

Play together.

Use Same device for pass-and-play, or Play online to share an invitation. Both online players must agree to a rematch. Keep the host’s tab open; a guest can refresh and rejoin the same room, including during a pending capture.

Keyboard: Tab or arrow keys to navigate; Enter or Space to act; Escape to deselect. Piece symbols and text identify players as well as color.

Rules reference: Masters Traditional Games. The original three-piece Java game is retained in the repository as historical source.

NO ACCOUNT. NO CAMERA.

How online play works.

GitHub Pages serves this website. PeerJS’s public signaling service introduces the browsers; WebRTC carries the game messages. PeerJS 1.5.5 includes public STUN/TURN defaults to help with network restrictions.

The connection library is downloaded from jsDelivr, or unpkg as a fallback, only for online play. These services can see network metadata such as your IP address. Your opponent may also learn your IP address through WebRTC. Share invites privately and play with people you trust.

No analytics, login, microphone or camera. Local games use browser storage. A guest rejoin token uses tab session storage. Online room state stays in host memory: closing or refreshing the host tab ends the room. Older three-piece saves and rooms are kept separate from this edition.

Public services have no availability guarantee. Restrictive networks can still block a connection; try another network. Same-device play does not need these services.

The host validates all moves and captures; guests replay the history. This is friendly peer-hosted play, not a cheat-proof ranked service.
